Design of Innovative Learning Environments

Programming, innovation and reflection on teaching practice in science

design
innovation
learning
Subject focused on the design and evaluation of learning situations and action research in the classroom
Author

Pedro Martinez Duran

Published

Wednesday, March 11, 2026

Modified

Friday, November 21, 2025

1 Presentation

The two key objectives of this subject are:

  • Give relevance to reflective practice in teaching and the teacher’s daily life as a method of transforming reality and improving teaching-learning processes.

  • Learn the process of designing and evaluating learning situations based on the current didactic model, and contextualized in the competency-based work curriculum.

  • The achievement of the first objective, which involves learning the systematization of the reflective process, will lead students to develop the role of teacher-researcher who promotes teaching innovation.

  • In the case of the second key objective of the subject, its achievement must allow students to be competent in the process of programming the different subjects and designing the corresponding sessions, essential to carry out the teaching task.

  • Some of the contents covered are continuation, expansion and application of aspects started during the first quarter in the subject of Fundamentals of Learning and Teaching of natural sciences.

  • The practical approach of the activities that the students will carry out will be prioritized, so that the final task of the subject will be the tutored preparation of two of the three final products of the master’s degree: a learning situation for ESO and a learning situation for high school, as well as the design and implementation of an action research in the classroom, which will be put into practice during the second phase of the practicum, which will allow the students to prepare their final master’s thesis (TFM). Whenever possible, students will develop, apply and evaluate the teaching materials and educational actions developed within the framework of these two final products in the classroom in pairs.


2 Technical Information

  • Subject code: 32439

  • Timing:

    • Full time: first and second semesters
    • Part time: second year, annual

  • Number of credits: 10

  • Dedication: 65 hours in person and 185 hours of autonomous work (250 hours total)

  • Teachers: Bernat Blasco, Marcel Costa, Eulàlia Durán, Laura Farró, Jèssica Fernández, Davínia Hernández-Leo, Sílvia Lope, Iván Marchán, Xavi Muñoz, Diego Sanjulián (coordination), Gemma Serra, Jordi Soler.



3 Subject Modules

  • Classroom observation

  • Design and evaluation of teaching sequences

  • Methodological bases for innovation and educational research

  • Innovation projects in scientific education


4 Goals

  • Prepare the programming of learning situations.

  • Design classroom materials to implement learning situations.

  • Analyze the results of the classroom implementation of the designed sessions.

  • Propose improvements to the weak points detected during the implementation of learning situations.

  • Identify aspects to improve educational practice through classroom observation

  • Analyze innovation projects in scientific education

  • Design an action research cycle to implement in the Practicum

  • Know the regulatory and organizational framework of Vocational Training in Catalonia. Interpret and apply the curricular elements of the FP in the planning and design of programs.


5 Competencies

  1. Design, management and planning of science teaching-learning environments

  2. Communication and personal interaction in teaching-learning processes and effective involvement in teaching teams

  3. Teaching methodology and diversity management in the classroom

  4. Research, innovation and reflection on professional identity and the improvement of teaching practice

  5. Use of digital environments for professional development and educational interaction


6 Contents

6.1 Structure of the programming of a didactic sequence

  • Defining the sequence approach and learning context

  • Forecasting possible obstacles

  • Description of the production that students must carry out

  • Relationship of the contents, their progression and their relationship with other subjects and other parts of the subject

  • Writing objectives

  • Concretion of the global, transversal, specific competencies of the scientific-technological field and SDGs that will be worked on

  • Definition of success indicators and evaluation criteria

  • Description of the sequence of activities, their classroom management, timing and evaluation instruments

  • Specification of measures to address diversity, provision of necessary resources and connections with other subjects

6.2 Action research in the classroom

  • Observation in the classroom: objectives, observation models and instruments.

  • The interrelationship between practice, observation, educational research and innovation.

  • Action research cycles and their relationship with one’s own training as a teacher.

  • Current trends in science education research.

  • Design and evaluation of didactic innovations.

  • Design of information collection instruments for educational research.

  • Analysis and interpretation of data in educational research.


7 Methodology

7.1 a) Theoretical-practical sessions

  • This subject has a very practical nature and is closely related to the practicum. The classes are designed so that students incorporate knowledge into their teaching practices.

  • The sessions will be of a practical-theoretical nature, which means that students will be constantly confronted with concrete learning experiences and activities, from the reflection of which theoretical knowledge will gradually be built.

  • In several of the face-to-face sessions, accompanied work will be carried out to prepare the programming, materials and didactic interventions, which make up the final products of the subject and the master’s degree (a learning situation in high school, a learning situation for ESO and an action research of didactic innovation), which will subsequently be taken to the classroom during the second phase of the practicum.

  • The internship work groups, that is, those students who will design and apply these products together, will be formed from the beginning of the master’s degree and based on the allocation of places to carry out the internships at the educational center.

7.2 b) Non-face-to-face tasks

  • Students will have various documents at their disposal on the virtual Platform. A good part of these tasks will be directed towards the elaboration of the different parts of the high school didactic sequence and the ESO unit. The other non-face-to-face tasks, which will be prepared in the students’ e-portfolio, will allow the action research cycle aimed at introducing a didactic innovation to be carried out: presentation and discussion of the inquiry question, analysis of an article that bases the innovation and presentation of the action plan.

7.3 c) Oral presentations

  • At the end of the third quarter of the master’s degree, after having experienced the high school learning situation and the ESO learning situation in the classroom, and carrying out the action research in the classroom, an oral presentation of the results will be given. In these presentations, students must make a synthesis of the didactic characteristics and activities of the ESO unit, as well as a special emphasis on the analysis of the results obtained and the proposals for improvement that they consider relevant. Likewise, this presentation will present the results and conclusions obtained from the didactic innovation introduced during the action research.


8 Assessment

  • Analysis and presentation of an educational research article (10%)

  • Programming and classroom materials for the ESO teaching unit (25%)

  • Programming and classroom materials of the BAT didactic sequence (25%)

  • Presentation of the action plan design (20%)

  • Oral presentation of the ESO teaching unit and the results and conclusions of the action research (20%)
