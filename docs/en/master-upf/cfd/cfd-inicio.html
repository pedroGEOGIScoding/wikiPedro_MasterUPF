Disciplinary Training Complements

Integrated guide to the Natural Sciences specialty (Biology and Geology / Physics and Chemistry)

accessories
discipline
Subject that complements disciplinary training in natural sciences from an integrated and disciplinary perspective
Author

Pedro Martinez Duran

Published

Wednesday, March 11, 2026

Modified

Friday, November 21, 2025

1 Presentation

  • This subject has the purpose of complementing the disciplinary training of students in the field of natural sciences. Starting from the analysis of secondary education curricula, we will analyze how to approach science teaching from a perspective that is both integrated and disciplinary.

  • The main objective of this subject is to provide complementary scientific training that completes the knowledge of the bachelor’s degree or entry level to the master’s degree.


2 Technical Information

  • Subject code: 31543

  • Timing:

    • Full time: second semester
    • Part time: first year, annual

  • Number of credits: 5

  • Dedication: 35 hours in person and 90 hours of autonomous work (125 hours total)

  • Teachers: Xavier Muñoz (coordination), Iván Marchán, Laia Ramon-Sala, Gemma Viscasillas



3 Subject Modules

  • Resume analysis

  • Biology

  • Geology

  • Physics

  • Chemistry

Note: Each student must take two of the four proposed disciplinary blocks (Biology, Geology, Physics and Chemistry).


4 Goals

  • Know the key scientific models of the scientific disciplines of the specialty that do not correspond to the degree obtained.

  • Understand the most relevant conceptual errors related to the key models of science.

  • Know and apply the most efficient teaching strategies so that students can deal with the most relevant conceptual errors related to the key models of science.


5 Competencies

  1. Design, management and planning of science teaching-learning environments

  2. Communication and personal interaction in teaching-learning processes and effective involvement in teaching teams

  3. Teaching methodology and diversity management in the classroom

  4. Use of digital environments for professional development and educational interaction

7 Methodology

  • The sessions will be theoretical-practical, activities to work in the classroom will be combined with presentations by the teachers. Active learning methodologies will therefore predominate and reflective practice will be facilitated.

  • The student will have the materials used in classes at their disposal on the virtual platform.


8 Assessment

8.1 Task 1: Analysis of the curricula (25%)

  • This is a group activity. This activity aims to analyze the ESO science curriculum, it will be carried out in groups and will be evaluated through an oral presentation that each of the groups will make.

8.2 Task 2: Assessment of disciplinary knowledge (35%)

  • Knowledge of each discipline will be assessed through the completion of a collaborative exam.

Phase 1: Each student must individually take a written test. The student must answer in writing the questions related to the two blocks they have taken. Teachers will collect the written work.

Phase 2: Immediately following phase 1, groups of 4-5 students will be formed (according to the teachers’ instructions). Each group will share and argue the individual answers and will answer the questions again in a single document that the teachers will also collect.

Phase 3: Each student will work individually again and do a metacognitive regulation.

8.3 Task 3: Evaluation of didactic knowledge of scientific disciplines (30%)

  • In groups formed by students who take the same block.

  • Each group must write a proposal for a teaching-learning activity on the topic and level indicated, consistent with the secondary school curriculum and that mobilizes the contents and resources worked on in the corresponding disciplinary block. It will also be necessary to indicate the criteria that would be used to evaluate the students.
