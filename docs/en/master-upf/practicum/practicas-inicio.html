Practicum

Internships in Educational Center

practicum
observation
assessment
competencies
Acquisition of skills related to professional teaching practice
Author

Pedro Martínez

Published

Wednesday, March 11, 2026

Modified

Friday, November 21, 2025

1 Presentation

  • The main purpose of the practicum of the Master of Secondary Education Teacher Training is the acquisition of skills related to professional practice, in addition to being a space to put into practice and synthesize in action the knowledge acquired in the other subjects. The practicum must involve professional construction experience.

  • The practicum is dedicated to observation and participation in a secondary school and to reflection on its reality and on the student’s educational action. This is the first contact with the profession, and it is the time to recognize models of action, contrast theoretical reflections with real practice, as well as to ask questions about one’s own ideas and skills.

  • We must be aware that we are witnessing the reality of the activity of teaching and learning, not a sample of what would be ideal, of perfection; that the mentors and the rest of the professionals that you will find in the centers are “people” with their personal, family, and work problems.

  • It is important to take advantage of the opportunity offered to us to learn about and reflect on aspects that should be improved in educational centers. You will be the future professionals, who will soon find yourself practicing in a center like the one that now welcomes you as observers; You are, therefore, the first interested in taking advantage of all the knowledge that you will acquire in this course to write evaluations and reflections that help us make and apply proposals for improvement to the educational system.


2 Technical Information

  • Subject code: 32018

  • Timing:

    • Full time: first and second semesters
    • Part time: second year, annual

  • Number of credits: 14

  • Dedication: 250 hours of work in the internship center, 20 hours of practicum seminars and 185 hours of autonomous work (455 hours total)

  • Responsible teacher: Marcel Costa (coordination), mentors from the internship centers and tutors from the university



3 Subject Modules

  • Classroom observation

  • Integration as a member of a Senate

  • Implementation of teaching sequences

  • Application of didactic innovations

  • Reflection on one’s own practice and the results obtained

  • Practicum Seminars


4 Goals

  • Know the operation and organization of secondary schools.

  • Follow and analyze the day-to-day life of a secondary school science teacher.

  • Actively participate in the various activities carried out in an educational center.

  • Integrate into the Faculty, teaching teams and science department of an educational center.

  • Help the mentor in specific tasks of management and correction of teaching activities.

  • Agree with the mentor on the topics of the didactic sequence and unit to be designed in the DEIA subject.

  • Implement the designed sequence and teaching unit, observe its results and propose improvements.

  • Contrast with the mentor the results of the teaching activities carried out and apply the suggestions for improvement received.

  • Carry out educational interventions linked to the TFM.

  • Compile data and observations derived from the action research carried out in the context of the TFM.


5 Competencies

5.1 1. Knowledge and participation in the educational center

  • Knowledge of the social and cultural reality of the center

  • Search for information about the educational project and the operation of the center

  • Active participation in the life of the center as a member of the Faculty

  • Communication and adequate relationship with all members of the educational community

5.2 2. Performance within the classroom

  • Frame didactic actions based on the context of each group and the functioning of the educational center

  • Constructive interaction with students to promote the participation of all

  • Temperate and respectful attitude in the classroom to facilitate positive conflict resolution

  • Have a good knowledge of the students and maintain an appropriate classroom climate

  • Communicative ability at a verbal and non-verbal level with students

  • Use of appropriate teaching resources for each teaching activity

5.3 3. Design and implementation of learning situations

  • Knowledge of the contents and adequate structuring of learning activities (contextualized and competency-based)

  • Plan formative and training evaluation processes.

  • Create documents, materials and work resources for students in a creative, appropriate and coherent manner.

  • Use classroom methodologies appropriate to the context of the students and the didactic intention.

  • Sufficient teaching digital competence in relation to the intervention carried out in the classroom

  • Manage time appropriately both in the design and implementation of teaching activities.

  • Consider democratic and universal values ​​agreed upon at the social level (sustainability, commitment and social justice, equity, etc.)

  • Reflect on the development of the classroom sessions and the feedback received from your mentor, particularly in terms of student learning, to build practical knowledge that allows improvements in the future.


6 Work Plan

The practicum will be distributed in two periods and will be structured in three phases:

  • Observation phase (25 hours of dedication)

  • Accompanied intervention phase (75 hours of dedication)

  • Autonomous intervention phase (150 hours of dedication)

The first phase and part of the second will be carried out during the first period of the practicum. The final part of the second phase and the entirety of the third will be carried out during the second period of the practicum.

6.1 Meetings with the mentor

  • At the beginning of each of the phases, students will meet with their mentor and the Center’s practicum coordinator to plan the tasks they will carry out during this stage. The planning must include all the tasks required in the practicum work plan.

6.2 Observation phase

  • This period has the purpose of making a global analysis of the center, its model of educational and management practice and its relationship with the environment, as well as establishing a first contact with the life of the center and the classroom.

Specific tasks that must be performed during this phase:

  • Attend the mentor’s classes.

  • Attend Department coordination meetings.

  • Attend coordination meetings of teachers from different areas of the same level.

  • Consult the mentor about what topics the learning situations can be developed.

6.3 After the observation phase

  • After the observation phase, you must begin to design a teaching sequence of at least 4-5 sessions for high school (2-3 if you work individually) and a complete teaching unit for ESO, of a minimum of 8 sessions (6 sessions if you work individually), which will be taught during the autonomous intervention phase.

6.4 Accompanied intervention phase

  • In this period, the student begins by carrying out specific interventions in the classroom with the support and supervision of the mentor and the monitoring and reflection of the master’s tutor. In addition to the specific tasks assigned, the student must actively participate in the mentor’s classes and, if possible, in others at the center. During the first days of this second phase (still in the first quarter of the course), the student will agree with his mentor on the specific topic of the programming units, which he will design and apply later (autonomous intervention phase). Also during this phase you must know and become familiar with the groups of students in which you will take your classes during the autonomous intervention phase.

Specific tasks that must be done during this phase:

  • Propose and teach a minimum of two activities in the mentor’s classes and assess their development. The student must select a minimum of two activities from all the resources that have been provided in the teaching specialization module and adapt them to the mentor’s classes. If possible, it is recommended that the two activities be diverse, such as experimental practices, TAC activities, debate activities, role-playing games, among others.

  • Participate in an outing outside the classroom if it coincides with the internship calendar.

  • Correct some of the exercises that the students have done: Agree with the mentor on the correction criteria. Make an analysis of the results obtained. Carry out a training return to the students.

  • Attend evaluation meetings (if possible)

  • Attend Department coordination meetings

  • Attend coordination meetings of teachers from different areas of the same level.

  • Participate in some tutoring sessions with the entire class group.

  • Attend a tutor coordination meeting

  • Attend some meetings with families. (if possible)

  • Attend some individualized tutoring meetings with students.

  • Agree with the mentor, before the end of the first period of stay at the center, on the contents to be taught in the third phase of the practicum, that is, the specific topics of the high school teaching sequence and the ESO teaching unit.

6.5 Autonomous intervention phase

  • The main objective of this phase is to bring to the classroom the didactic sequences that have been designed in agreement with your mentor and the university tutor, with everything that entails: monitoring, evaluation and acting as a teacher responsible for the class group, making the corresponding decisions to manage the classroom.

  • In addition to the implementation of these didactic sequences, throughout this phase, the student will continue to participate and collaborate in the same type of activities that they developed throughout the accompanied intervention phase (attendance at meetings, attending activities outside the center, helping the mentor with correction tasks, etc.). Likewise, it will carry out those actions that allow it to obtain data from the action research cycle linked to the preparation of the final master’s thesis (TFM).


7 The Portfolio

7.1 Characteristics

  • The portfolio is a collection of information and documentation representative of a learning process. It is a training instrument that includes analysis and reflection on the actions that have been carried out and the design of improvement plans, basic elements that enable self-learning and continued training in the professional future.

  • At the same time, it is also an instrument of accreditation, since it allows the person who is learning to show what they have learned and how they have learned it in a very personal and complete way, documenting it with representative material.

  • During the practicum, the student must prepare a portfolio that, as its main objective, must show the development of the teaching professional’s basic skills, which he or she will put into practice during his or her stay at the teaching center. The portfolio is a good instrument to facilitate and demonstrate the development of these skills, both in the initial training period you are in, and in your future practice and professional promotion.

  • The portfolio presentation format will be electronic and will be done with a Google site.

  • In this link you can copy the pre-established structure from a template. A document will be posted in the virtual classroom with all the details of the portfolio startup procedure and its functionalities.

7.2 Elaboration

  • The portfolio will be made during the two periods of stay at the internship center, with deliveries at different times, which will correspond to the tasks that will be carried out in each phase of the practicum according to the specific work plan in each case. You must have completed the entire portfolio at the end of the autonomous intervention phase.

7.3 Structure

The portfolio will collect different tasks that are carried out with the support of different subjects:

Tasks Subject where you work
1. My Practicum center Practicum (seminars)
2. The story of the Practicum Practicum (seminars)
3. The Practicum inquiry journal DEIA
4. The two learning situations DEIA
5. A video recording of the student’s intervention in the classroom, self-assessed and co-assessed by a classmate Practicum (seminars)
6. A personal assessment of how basic teaching skills have been developed during the practicum Practicum (seminars)

7.4 1. My practice center

  • During the observation phase, a brief description of the center where the Practicum is carried out must be included in the portfolio. Emphasis will be placed on the personal vision of the master’s student, answering the following questions: What image did you take away from the internship center during the observation phase? How is it similar to the center where you studied and how is it different?

7.5 2. The story of the Practicum

  • During the observation phase of the Practicum, a brief story will be given about a relevant event that has been experienced at the internship center, with the aim of becoming familiar with the use of narrative records for reflective practice. This will be analyzed in one of the Practicum seminars and will be incorporated into the portfolio, after having worked on it in class.

7.6 3. Inquiry diary

  • The inquiry diary will be carried out throughout the period of the practicum and has the objective of developing the reflective capacity of future teachers as a tool to better understand the teaching and learning processes, and know how to use this knowledge to improve their teaching practices and innovate. It will be done in electronic format, through a template available in the e-portfolio. The different phases of the diary will be worked on in the DEIA sessions.

7.7 4. The two learning situations

  • During the autonomous intervention phase, the two learning situations designed and implemented in the classroom during this practice phase must be attached to the portfolio.

7.8 5. Video recording of the intervention in the classroom

  • During the intervention phase of the practicum, it will be necessary to record some class sessions, those that seem most representative. At the end of this phase, some fragments of the recordings must be selected, whether they are good or problematic moments, that are representative of communication and constructive interaction in the classroom and of the didactic model and methodological options. It’s not just about showing the perfect moments, but also those that we would like to comment on. It is advisable to select between 3 and 5 moments of about 3-4 minutes and make an edit of a maximum of 15 minutes.

  • The videos will be worked on in the last seminar of the Practicum and must be attached to the portfolio along with a self-assessment and a co-assessment.

7.9 6. Personal assessment of the development of teaching skills

  • During the first seminar of the practicum, a table will be made to explain the initial personal assessment of teaching competencies. In the last seminar, based on the experiences lived in the second phase of practices, a contrast will be made between this initial assessment and the final vision of the degree of personal development of teaching competencies. This contrast will be guided by the practicum evaluation rubric and will have a maximum length of two pages. This writing must state:

What has been learned and how it has been learned. Refers to the specific aspects of each competence that have been learned during this period and the activity/s that have facilitated this learning.

The strong points or aspects that have been most achieved in each competition.

The weak points or aspects to improve in the future of each competition.

The attempts at improvement made. Refers to the improvements made during the months of the practicum.

Evidence to document the statements made in the competency assessment report. It is good to relate, above all, the strengths and improvements referred to with representative material that confirms them. This material can be material that has already been previously attached to the portfolio (resources used, learning situations, video recording) or any other evidence that you consider appropriate.


8 Evaluation, Monitoring and Feedback

  • The evaluation of the practicum will be carried out from the centers where the students carry out the two internship periods and by the university tutors. During the two phases of practicum, the mentor and students will sign a table weekly showing the tasks completed and the hours of attendance at the center.

8.1 e-Portfolio (50%)

  • It will be evaluated by the UPF tutor and the regularity of the contributions made will be taken into account overall, as well as the quality of the reflections in the different sections (see details in the specific section of the e-Portfolio). If at any time the portfolio does not contain all the materials required to demonstrate the achievement of teaching competencies, the practicum tutor will notify the student so that they can correct it.

  • The presentation of the portfolio will be a requirement to be able to pass the practicum and propose the Final Master’s Thesis (TFM).

  • Each student will have a tutor from the university, who will be in charge of monitoring the practicum and will provide support in preparing the portfolio. Throughout the practicum period there will be continuous monitoring of the tutor through the e-portfolio. In addition, there will be a series of sessions scheduled throughout the course, called Practicum Seminars where all aspects related to the Practicum and the tasks that must be incorporated into the e-portfolio will be discussed.

8.2 Internships in an educational center (50%)

  • They will be evaluated by the practicum mentor and will be based on the development of teaching competencies, described in the practicum evaluation rubric.

  • The university tutor will visit the student to observe how he or she carries out one or more class sessions during the second internship. This visit is intended to provide feedback to the student but does not have any weight in the overall grade of the practicum.
